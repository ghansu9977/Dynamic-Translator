Understanding CTC vs Gross Salary vs In-Hand Salary in India

When you receive a job offer letter, the total figure mentioned is typically the CTC (Cost to Company). Many professionals are surprised when their first paycheck is considerably lower than CTC / 12. This difference occurs because CTC includes indirect statutory expenses like Employer PF contributions, gratuity, and group insurance which do not reach your monthly bank account.

1. What is Cost to Company (CTC)?

CTC represents the entire annual expense an employer allocates to retain you. It comprises:

  • Direct Benefits (Gross Salary): Basic Salary, House Rent Allowance (HRA), Special Allowance, Medical/Conveyance allowances.
  • Indirect Benefits: Employer's 12% PF contribution, Gratuity allocation (4.81% of basic), and group health insurance.
  • Variable / Bonus Components: Annual performance-linked incentives or retention bonuses.

2. What is Net In-Hand (Take-Home) Salary?

In-Hand salary is the exact disposable liquid income deposited into your bank account on payday. The formula is:

In-Hand Salary = Gross Salary - (Employee PF + Professional Tax + Income Tax / TDS)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. How much is the in-hand salary for a ₹6 Lakh CTC?

Under the New Tax Regime, income tax is zero for a ₹6 Lakh CTC due to Section 87A rebate. After subtracting EPF (~₹28,800/year) and Professional Tax (~₹2,400/year), your monthly take-home is approximately ₹44,000 to ₹45,500 per month.

2. What is Professional Tax (PT)?

Professional Tax is a state-level levy collected by state governments (such as Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, West Bengal) capped at a maximum of ₹2,500 per annum (typically ₹200/month).

3. Can I opt out of EPF contributions?

If your Basic Salary exceeds ₹15,000 per month at the time of joining your first job, opting out of EPF is legally permitted if agreed by employer policy. However, most Indian corporates keep EPF mandatory for employee retirement benefits.
