In-Hand Salary Calculation Guide: Decoding CTC vs Gross vs Net Pay in India

By RozgarTools Finance Desk • Educational Guide • 8 min read

Receiving an attractive job offer letter with a headline Cost to Company (CTC) of ₹10,00,000 per annum is an exhilarating moment. However, when the first month's salary hits your bank account, many professionals are shocked to see a credit of ₹64,000 rather than the expected ₹83,333 (₹10 Lakhs ÷ 12).

Where did the remaining money go? The gap between annual CTC and monthly in-hand credit is governed by statutory laws, social security funds, and income tax brackets. In this guide, our financial analysts deconstruct every line-item of the Indian pay structure so you can forecast your exact take-home pay.

The Three Tiers: CTC vs Gross vs Net Pay

1. Cost to Company (CTC)

The total annual expense an employer incurs to retain you. It includes your gross salary PLUS employer retirement contributions (EPF matching, gratuity), variable bonuses, and corporate insurance cover.

2. Gross Salary

The total compensation earned before any taxes or personal deductions. It equals Basic Salary + House Rent Allowance (HRA) + Special Allowance + any monthly performance incentives.

3. Net (In-Hand) Pay

The actual money transferred to your savings account on payday. Calculated as: Gross Salary minus Employee EPF minus Professional Tax (PT) minus Monthly Income Tax (TDS).

Anatomy of Salary Components

Component Typical Proportion Tax Treatment
Basic Salary 40% to 50% of CTC 100% Taxable. Forms the base for EPF and Gratuity.
House Rent Allowance (HRA) 40% (non-metro) or 50% (metro) of Basic Exempt under Sec 10(13A) in Old Regime; fully taxable in New Regime.
Special Allowance Balancing figure (20% to 30%) 100% Taxable.
Employee EPF (12%) 12% of Basic Deducted from paycheck. Earns tax-free interest (under limits).
Professional Tax (PT) ₹200/month (varies by state) State levy capped at ₹2,500 annually.

New Tax Regime vs Old Tax Regime Overview

Under the New Tax Regime (default):

  • A flat Standard Deduction of ₹75,000 is provided to salaried personnel.
  • Under Section 87A rebate, individuals with total taxable income up to ₹7,00,000 pay zero income tax.
  • Tax brackets are streamlined (0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 30%), eliminating cumbersome investment proofs like 80C, 80D, or rent receipts.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

1. Why is my monthly in-hand salary significantly lower than my CTC divided by 12?

Cost to Company (CTC) includes non-monetary employer contributions such as Employer EPF (12%), Gratuity provision (4.81% of basic), annual performance bonuses, and health insurance premiums. When statutory employee EPF, professional tax, and monthly income tax (TDS) are deducted, the actual bank credit is typically 15% to 25% lower.

2. How is Employee Provident Fund (EPF) calculated?

EPF is calculated as 12% of your Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA). Your employer matches an equal 12% contribution, of which 8.33% is allocated toward the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) up to statutory wage caps.

3. What is the standard deduction benefit under the New Tax Regime?

Salaried employees receive a flat standard deduction of ₹75,000 from their gross taxable salary under the updated New Tax Regime, lowering overall taxable liability automatically without requiring investment proofs.
